\section{Formal Guarantees}
\label{sec:formal_guarantees}

\subsection{Notation and Assumptions}
We establish the following formal assumptions for QWeave's symbolic operation. Let $L$ be the set of logical qubits and $P$ the physical sites. The hardware connectivity is an undirected graph $G_H=(P, E_H)$. 

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \label{A:injective} \textbf{Injective Mapping}: The mapping $\pi: L \to P$ is strictly injective, obeying the convention $\pi(l) = p$, often written \texttt{mapping[logical] = physical}.
    \item \label{A:undirected} \textbf{Undirected Routing}: Routing searches for paths over undirected hardware edges.
    \item \label{A:contiguous} \textbf{Contiguous Labels}: Physical qubit labels are contiguous integers $0 \dots |P|-1$.
    \item \label{A:connected} \textbf{Connected Component}: The hardware $G_H$ is a connected graph with $|P| \ge |L|$.
    \item \label{A:gate_family} \textbf{Supported Gate Family}: The circuit consists of unconditioned one- and two-qubit unitaries, resets, and terminal measurements. Dynamic control and active mid-circuit classical conditioning are rejected.
    \item \label{A:durations} \textbf{Positive Durations}: Gate durations $\tau > 0$ are strictly positive.
    \item \label{A:global_phase} \textbf{Global Phase}: The global phase of the source is preserved across compilation.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Local-Search Termination]
The local-search mapper terminates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
The neighborhood size is finite. The algorithm only accepts moves that strictly decrease the objective function. Because the state space of permutations is finite and the sequence of accepted objective values is strictly monotonically decreasing, the search must terminate.
\end{proof}
\noindent \textbf{Verified by:} \texttt{tests/test\_proof\_prop1.py::test\_prop1\_local\_search\_strict\_descent\_and\_termination}

\begin{lemma}[SWAP Preserves Injectivity]
A legally inserted SWAP operation between two adjacent physical sites symmetrically updates the forward mapping $\pi$ and its inverse, strictly preserving injectivity (Assumption \ref{A:injective}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
A SWAP exchanges the logical occupants of physical sites $u$ and $v$. Because it is a bijection on the physical sites and updates the mapping symmetrically, two distinct logical qubits will never be assigned to the same physical site, preserving one-to-one correspondence.
\end{proof}
\noindent \textbf{Verified by:} \texttt{tests/test\_proof\_lemma1.py::test\_lemma1\_swaps\_preserve\_injectivity}

\begin{lemma}[Masked Softmax Probabilities]
The learned graph policy assigns strictly zero probability to illegal topological actions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
The environment yields a binary \texttt{action\_mask} based on current frontier legality. The Graph Actor--Critic applies a large negative logit to masked indices before softmax normalization, ensuring their probability is mathematically compressed to zero ($<10^{-12}$) and the remaining legal actions sum to one.
\end{proof}
\noindent \textbf{Verified by:} \texttt{tests/test\_proof\_lemma2.py::test\_lemma2\_masked\_softmax\_zero\_illegal}

\begin{theorem}[Hardware Legality]
Every two-qubit operation emitted by the router lies on an undirected edge of $G_H$ (Assumption \ref{A:undirected}). After direction-lowering synthesis, every resulting operation respects the strictly directed couplers of the physical device.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
The router emits source gates only when their mapped physical qubits are adjacent in $G_H$, and inserts SWAPs only along $G_H$ edges. The independent lowering pass explicitly synthesizes reverse-CNOTs and directed-SWAPs using only native one-way arcs.
\end{proof}
\noindent \textbf{Verified by:} \texttt{tests/test\_proof\_thm1.py::test\_thm1\_hardware\_legality\_undirected\_and\_directed}

\begin{theorem}[Semantic Equivalence]
Given Assumption \ref{A:gate_family} (supported gate family) and Assumption \ref{A:injective} ($\pi(l) = p$), the routed unitary circuit is semantically equivalent to the source circuit up to initial and final layout permutations. That is,
\begin{equation}
 U_{\mathrm{routed}} P_{\mathrm{initial}} = P_{\mathrm{final}} U_C.
 \label{eq:equivalence_theory}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
SWAP insertions are state-preserving permutations that update $\pi$. Applying the initial permutation matches the logical bases to the physical register. Executing the routed circuit applies the source unitaries along with permutation updates. Reversing the final physical permutation returns the state back to the original logical basis, matching the unitary operator of the original circuit.
\end{proof}
\noindent \textbf{Verified by:} \texttt{tests/test\_proof\_thm2.py::test\_thm2\_equivalence\_up\_to\_layout\_permutations\_equal\_width}

\begin{theorem}[Scheduler Feasibility]
For a fixed routed circuit, the list scheduler packs operations without violating physical-wire precedences, and no two operations overlapping in time share a physical qubit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
The scheduler maintains a strict per-wire directed dependency graph. By construction, an operation only enters the ready set when all its predecessors on all its physical qubits have finished. As a result, temporal overlaps on the same qubit are prohibited, and sequence order is strictly preserved.
\end{proof}
\noindent \textbf{Verified by:} \texttt{tests/test\_list\_scheduler.py::test\_critical\_path\_priority\_packs\_disjoint\_work\_without\_depth\_claim}

\begin{theorem}[Fallback Termination]
On a connected hardware graph (Assumption \ref{A:connected}), the sequential environment router with shortest-path fallback completes in finite steps.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
If the policy fails to make topological progress for a bounded number of steps, the environment forcibly interrupts the policy. It computes the deterministic shortest path in $G_H$ between the separated operands, emits the required SWAPs, and executes the pending gate, guaranteeing finite-time frontier progress.
\end{proof}
\noindent \textbf{Verified by:} \texttt{tests/test\_proof\_thm4.py::test\_thm4\_fallback\_completes\_on\_random\_connected\_graphs}

\begin{lemma}[Makespan Lower Bound]
For a \textbf{fixed routed circuit} with strictly positive durations (Assumption \ref{A:durations}), the schedule makespan is strictly bounded from below by the maximum of the critical-path duration and the maximum single-qubit summed load.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
Under a strictly per-wire precedence model, any execution path forms a chain of dependencies. The critical path is the longest such chain in the directed acyclic graph. Any individual qubit's sequence of operations also forms a valid temporal chain. Therefore, the total elapsed time must be at least the maximal path length.
\end{proof}
\noindent \textbf{Verified by:} \texttt{tests/test\_scheduler\_verification.py::test\_makespan\_bounds\_always\_hold}

\subsection{What These Results Do Not Establish}
While the above symbolic and local checks guarantee strict physical legality and equivalence of the generated routing sequences, they do not imply:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Global optimality of the generated SWAP paths or layout mapping.
    \item PPO training convergence, or any advantage over deterministic SABRE routing.
    \item Improved physical device fidelity (as hardware noise calibration is absent).
    \item Support for dynamic control flow or active mid-circuit classical conditioning.
    \item Guarantee of success on disconnected hardware targets where fallback fails.
\end{itemize}
